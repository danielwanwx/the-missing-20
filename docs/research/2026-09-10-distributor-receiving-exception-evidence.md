# Distributor receiving exceptions beyond late cartons

**Research date:** 2026-09-10 (PDT)<br>
**Scope:** A bounded review of primary standards, government procedures, manufacturer requirements, carrier claims guidance, and official ERP documentation. No prevalence estimate is inferred. The four scenarios below are explicitly synthetic examples derived from the control patterns in the sources.

## Decision

A receiving observation is a trigger to investigate, not proof of a cause. A carton can have the expected outer count and a readable label while its inner quantity, part revision, lot identity, or quality is wrong. A clean exterior can coexist with a hidden defect. The receiving system should preserve the observation, join it to authoritative purchasing and traceability records, hold only the affected scope, and request a physical count, inspection, or test before release. For an exception, a person with the required authority records the disposition; a normal conforming path may auto-release under a standing policy once its required checks and acceptance criteria pass.

The evidence boundary is deliberate:

- An external photograph can show what is visible in that frame: exterior condition, a seal, a label, or a legible code. It cannot prove the count or identity of hidden inner units, a hidden functional or dimensional defect, authenticity, causation, or which customers are affected.
- GS1 models nested trade-item and logistic-unit relationships, including parent and child identifiers, but a relationship in a record is not a live count of the contents. NASA's receiving procedure calls for opening packages where appropriate and checking part number and quantity against the purchasing documents. See [GS1 General Specifications](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/genspecs/24.0.0/) and [NASA NPR 4100.1F, Chapter 6](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_4100_001F_&page_name=Chapter6).
- A readable code is an observation, not a conformity or authenticity decision. JPL's certificate requirements call for part, quantity, lot/batch/serial traceability, specification and revision, acceptance criteria, actual results, and test-instrument data where applicable; a label photo cannot supply those facts by itself. See [JPL QC14b](https://supplierportal.jpl.nasa.gov/qcprint.php?clause=QC14b).
- A damage claim also needs retained packaging and contents and may need an inspection report. FedEx's [claim requirements](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/customer-support/claims.html) illustrate why an outside photograph alone should create a claim or inspection task, rather than an automatic finding about the product inside.

## Actionable exception matrix

The `possible causes` column contains hypotheses to test, not attribution. The `provable cause` column states the evidence needed to move beyond the signal.

| Failure pattern | Observed signal | Possible causes vs. provable cause | Required evidence | Automatic workflow or alert | Human physical evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Inner-pack shortage with a correct outer-carton count** | PO/ASN and outer case or SSCC scans agree, but an opened case has fewer inner packs or units than the packaging master; a receipt conversion says 10 while a tally says 9. | **Possible:** pack-out/pick omission, tampering or repack, a separated damaged inner pack, or a stale packaging/UOM conversion. **Provable:** a signed open-and-count record tied to parent/child IDs, the PO/ASN/packing list, seals and custody, and any carrier inspection. Do not assign supplier or carrier fault from a photo. | Parent and child GTIN/SSCC scans; count per case; opened-content and packaging photos; witness, time, location, seal/tamper note; scale evidence if quantity is weight-based. | Put the affected line or parent unit on quantity hold; do not post missing units; create a supplier/carrier discrepancy task and request a recount or claim. Keep the affected packaging together. | Open the case, count or scan every relevant inner unit, tally and sign it, and preserve packaging and contents for inspection. NASA and GS1 support this separation of outer observation from inner verification. |
| **Wrong part, revision, or UOM** | A scan or inner marking differs from the PO, approved drawing, or certificate; revision/date code is unexpected; one box is interpreted as one each; physical count and conversion disagree. | **Possible:** an unapproved substitution, label mix-up, engineering change not synchronized to purchasing, or stale item-master conversion. **Provable:** the approved PO/drawing/revision and certificate agree with the inspected part; the relevant serial/lot is scanned; quantity is physically counted and the conversion is verified. | PO and approved revision; CoC/inspection certificate; inner part markings, serials/lots and UDI/GTIN where applicable; item-master conversion; measured count, dimensions, weight, or functional result. | Hard-hold the receipt and route it to procurement plus engineering/quality; create a nonconformance; never silently substitute a part or apply a disputed conversion. | Read the inner marking, count/measure the contents, and obtain an authorized engineering or quality disposition. JPL's [NDI/NDT evidence requirements](https://supplierportal.jpl.nasa.gov/qcprint.php?clause=QC14b) show why revision, quantity, traceability, criteria, and results belong in the record. |
| **Mixed or mislabeled batches** | One case or pallet says lot L7 while inner labels show L7 and L8, an unknown or duplicate lot, inconsistent expiry, or a lot assignment different from the ASN/receipt. | **Possible:** consolidation/repack, line-clearance failure, label swap, or a scan posted to the wrong lot. **Provable:** each affected child lot/serial is scanned and related to the parent event; supplier release/CoA, expiry, prior movements, and physical segregation agree. | Child lot/serial list; parent-child aggregation event; supplier CoA/release; expiry; receipt and movement history; sampling plan and results; quarantine location. | Quarantine the affected parent and linked lot scope until the sampling or traceability decision is made; create a lot-specific quality/nonconformance task; fan out to allocations and shipments only where lot/serial links prove impact. | Open and segregate the material, inspect the relevant inner labels and seals, and have quality sign the disposition. GS1's identifiers and FDA's package-level traceability model help join records; neither removes the physical check. |
| **Visible damage versus a measured quality failure** | The exterior is crushed, wet, punctured, or tampered; or the exterior looks clean but a dimension, electrical, contamination, or functional check fails. | **Possible:** transit shock/moisture, inadequate packaging, a pre-existing manufacturing defect, cosmetic-only damage, or hidden damage. **Provable:** packaging and contents, carrier exception/inspection, a defined test method and acceptance criteria, instrument readings, and the affected lot/serial. A photograph cannot choose among those causes. | Time-stamped exterior and interior photos; packaging and contents retained; carrier report; test procedure, instrument and readings; lot/serial; retained sample; release or rejection authority. | Start a damage claim and quarantine for visible damage; create a quality order/nonconformance for a test failure; treat “no visible damage” as only that observation, never as “fit for use.” | Preserve the original packaging and contents, then open, measure or test with suitable equipment and record the authorized disposition. JPL and [ISO documented-information guidance](https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/standards/docs/en/iso_9001_2015_guidance_documented_information.pdf) support objective measurement and disposition records. |
| **Counterfeit or traceability anomaly** | A UDI/GTIN/serial, where applicable, is unknown, duplicated, inconsistent with the label, or disconnected from the claimed manufacturer, lot, seal, or route. | **Possible:** counterfeit, diversion, relabel/repack, a registry lag, or data-entry error. **Provable:** independent manufacturer or registry verification, chain-of-custody and transaction records, security-feature comparison, and—where needed—manufacturer or laboratory examination. An unrecognized code alone does not prove counterfeit. | Machine-readable and plain-text identifiers; manufacturer/registry lookup; lot/serial and expiry; purchase and custody records; security-feature photos; retained physical sample; investigation and determination record. | Quarantine the suspect scope and stop release or allocation; open a traceability investigation; follow the regulated product's notification and disposition policy after the competent determination. Do not auto-clear a readable barcode. | Inspect the physical package, seal, serial and sample against manufacturer evidence; quality/regulatory personnel decide suspect, cleared, or illegitimate. See FDA [DSCSA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-supply-chain-integrity/drug-supply-chain-security-act), [UDI Basics](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/unique-device-identification-system-udi-system/udi-basics), SKF's [brand-protection account](https://evolution.skf.com/sourcing-genuine-products-concerns-everyone-everywhere/), and Schaeffler's [customer case](https://www.schaeffler.com/remotemedien/media/_shared_media/08_media_library/01_publications/schaeffler_2/global_technology_solution/downloads_28/customer_success_story_0162_de_en.pdf). |
| **Customer allocation and downstream shipment impact** | Available quantity falls below a commitment, or a held/wrong lot overlaps an allocation, sales order, pick, delivery, or shipment; the same lot/serial appears in an outbound record. | **Possible:** a physical shortage or quality hold, wrong lot, reservation drift, or a priority change. **Provable:** item/lot/location on-hand and held quantities join exactly to allocation/reservation, sales-order, pick, ship, and POD events with timestamps. A PO alone does not prove customer impact. | Physical on-hand, quarantine and released quantities; allocation/reservation; sales order and delivery; pick/ship/POD; exact lot/serial links; event times; customer and quantity affected. | Ring-fence held stock; calculate affected open allocations and already shipped customers; block further release; alert customer service, procurement, and quality. Do not auto-reallocate quarantined stock. Treat Microsoft allocation as planning/ring-fencing only: its [allocation documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/inventory/inventory-visibility-allocation) says allocation is a soft pool and does not itself set aside physical inventory. | Recount and verify physical segregation. An operations or customer-impact owner sets priority, substitute, expedite, or notification for impacted commitments; quality disposition controls any exception release. Normal released inventory follows the standing allocation policy. |

## Reusable workflow shape

1. **Capture the observation.** Store photo(s), scan values, time, location, receiver, package/parent ID, and a precise statement such as “no visible exterior damage in this frame.” Keep signal, hypothesis, and conclusion in separate fields.
2. **Join authoritative records.** Compare PO/ASN/packing list, item/revision/UOM, child lot/serial, certificate and quality criteria. Use parent-child identifiers for the join; do not treat a packaging relationship as a physical count.
3. **Scope the hold.** Quarantine the affected line, parent, lot, or serial set. Expand to linked allocations or shipments only when the traceability join demonstrates that they are affected.
4. **Request the missing physical evidence.** Count, open, inspect, measure, test, or retain the package and sample according to the applicable procedure. A system may create the task and checklist; it cannot turn an exterior image into hidden evidence.
5. **Resolve under standing policy.** A normal conforming path may auto-release after required measurements or checks meet configured acceptance criteria. For an exception, concession, recall investigation, or expanded downstream impact, record the authorized disposition, evidence, and timestamp; the software should not require a manager click for every passing unit. Preserve the raw observation and the decision record.

This maps to reusable controls already available in official ERP documentation: [ERPNext Quality Inspection](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/quality-inspection) can gate an incoming receipt with numeric, nonnumeric, or formula checks; [ERPNext Batch](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/batch) carries batch identity through stock transactions; and [purchasing in a different UOM](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/purchasing-in-different-unit) makes the conversion explicit. Microsoft Dynamics 365 documents quality associations that can start on purchase receipt, sample or block inventory, and create a nonconformance linked to a vendor, PO, lot, or customer-side order in [quality management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/inventory/quality-management-processes). These are workflow anchors, not claims that any one deployment has been configured to perform every action automatically.

The FDA DSCSA and UDI material is a regulated-product analogy, not a universal requirement for industrial parts distributors. Apply only the identifiers and notification obligations that govern the product, contract, and jurisdiction; the evidence pattern—identity, custody, verification, quarantine, and authorized disposition—generalizes more broadly.

## Firsthand component-company evidence

These are source-reported anecdotes and procedures, included to ground the exception patterns in actual component-company work. They are not independent prevalence evidence.

- In [SKF's field account](https://evolution.skf.com/sourcing-genuine-products-concerns-everyone-everywhere/), a brand-protection manager recounts a South American customer with recurring machine failures. A warehouse audit found one counterfeit bearing type among otherwise genuine stock, and the failed bearing matched that type. SKF also describes a mining customer whose counterfeit bearings failed in service and caused secondary damage. The operational lesson is to connect the failed physical part, warehouse stock check, supplier route, and specialist verification; a clean box photo would not establish that chain.
- In [Schaeffler's customer success story](https://www.schaeffler.com/remotemedien/media/_shared_media/08_media_library/01_publications/schaeffler_2/global_technology_solution/downloads_28/customer_success_story_0162_de_en.pdf), authorized Turkish sales partner Özevren Rulman suspected unusually cheap tendered bearings, offered customers free inspections, used the OriginCheck app and photos to start authentication, then informed customers and supplied investigation reports after several counterfeit transactions were uncovered. This is a concrete escalation path from suspicion to manufacturer verification and customer communication, not a claim that a photo alone proves authenticity.

## Four synthetic demo scenarios

These are **SYNTHETIC** examples for a receiving-exception demo. Numbers, IDs, and outcomes are illustrative and do not describe a real incident.

| Scenario | Evidence-led behavior |
|---|---|
| **SYNTHETIC A — Outer count right, inner count short** | The PO/ASN calls for two cases of 10 kits. Both outer SSCCs scan and the dock count is two cases. Opening one case finds nine kits. The system shows `QUANTITY_MISMATCH` and holds that parent; it records the outer photo as visible evidence, asks for the signed inner tally and packaging retention, and creates a supplier/carrier task. It does not receive 20 kits or claim who caused the shortage until the count and custody record exist. |
| **SYNTHETIC B — Revision and UOM conflict** | The PO expects `DRV-204`, Rev C, with one box equal to 20 each. The outer label reads Rev C, but an inner scan finds three Rev B units and the item master conversion is 1 box = 10. The system raises `PART_OR_UOM_REVIEW`, stops the receipt, and routes engineering/quality/procurement. A human checks the drawing, certificate, inner markings, count, and conversion before an authorized disposition. |
| **SYNTHETIC C — Mixed lot with a clean exterior** | A pallet label says L7; opened cartons contain L7 and L8. The exterior photo is clean, but an approved sample from L8 fails a dimension/continuity check. The system quarantines the affected parent and lot scope, creates a quality/nonconformance record, and computes downstream impact only for exact L8 links. It never treats the clean photo as a quality pass. |
| **SYNTHETIC D — Traceability anomaly with customer impact** | A serial/UDI lookup is unknown or duplicated. Four units are allocated to Customer A and two units with the same lot already appear in a shipment to Customer B. The system holds the suspect scope, stops new release, and shows the allocation and shipped links for human review. Quality or the manufacturer determines whether this is a data error, diversion, or illegitimate product; the system does not assert “counterfeit” from the scan failure or reallocate held stock automatically. |
